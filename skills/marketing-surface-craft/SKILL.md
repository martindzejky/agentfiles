# Marketing surface craft

Build around what the audience needs to understand, believe, and do. Project documents, the approved brief, and established brand choices take precedence over generic aesthetic advice. Accessibility and honest claims remain requirements in every visual direction.

## Start with the material

Read the brief, existing site, and relevant brand, design, and marketing documents. Inventory actual copy, product screenshots, photography, logos, evidence, and destinations for actions. Know what exists before composing a page around it.

For a new direction, identify the promise, audience, strongest available proof, and next action. Decide the visual character from the subject. Tune density, compositional variation, and motion to that direction; a redesign does not automatically need more motion or novelty.

## Compose from content

- Make the opening communicate what this is and why it matters. A headline, image, demonstration, or interaction can lead; choose the form that best represents the subject.
- Keep the main action easy to find. Balance type and asset scale so the opening works with real copy on both narrow and wide screens. Do not force every hero into a fixed line count or one-viewport mold.
- Give each section a job: explain, demonstrate, substantiate, answer an objection, or support an action. Remove sections that repeat the promise without adding evidence.
- Use consistent treatments for comparable items. Vary composition when the content changes, not merely to avoid repetition. A grid, list, table, or repeated row may be exactly right.
- Use labels, dividers, cards, and numbering for actual relationships. Eyebrows above every heading and meaningless section numbers add noise.
- Keep navigation understandable and usable as space changes. Never hide essential content or force a carousel just to make the page shorter.

Do not preselect a split hero, three cards, a bento section, a testimonial wall, and a final CTA as a universal page outline.

## Assets and evidence

Use relevant real assets when they help someone understand the product or trust the work. Check crop, resolution, focal point, mobile treatment, and loading behavior. A strong typographic direction need not meet an image quota.

Product screenshots and demonstrations should reflect actual capabilities. Clearly distinguish an illustrative concept from a working product. Use placeholders honestly while an asset is missing; do not manufacture customers, endorsements, statistics, or results. Preserve source attribution and usage rights where required.

Choose visuals for a purpose: a product detail can explain quality; a real workflow can substantiate a feature; a project image can carry a portfolio entry. Decorative media should not displace the evidence a visitor came to see.

## Visual system

Use existing tokens and components. When establishing a system, give color, type, spacing, shape, and depth clear roles. Keep actions recognizable; status colors and multiple meaningful data colors need not obey a “single accent” aesthetic rule.

Typography and spacing carry hierarchy before ornament. Use borders, shadows, and containers to clarify grouping rather than wrapping every block. Neither rounded cards nor sharp corners are inherently better. Avoid defaulting to cream/serif/clay, black/neon, or purple glow for unrelated subjects.

Motion should explain an action or support a particular story beat. Keep important content available without waiting for an entrance animation; avoid scroll hijacking and repetitive reveals. Respect reduced motion. Add another theme only when requested or already supported by the product.

## Copy and actions

Write specific headings and useful supporting text in the brand's voice. Match detail to the decision: a complex purchase may need substantial explanation, not an arbitrary word limit.

Name actions consistently and make destinations clear. Check every link, CTA, form submission, and relevant success/error state. Labels must remain visible; placeholders are not form labels. Preserve legal, consent, and pricing information rather than treating it as expendable visual clutter.

## Redesign with preservation

Establish whether the task is a targeted refresh or an approved overhaul. Audit the current brand, page structure, content, conversion paths, and patterns worth keeping before replacing them.

Unless the brief authorizes changes, preserve URLs, navigation meaning, form behavior, product claims, legal/consent copy, and brand recognition. A visual refresh does not imply a copy rewrite or information-architecture change. Check existing focus, keyboard navigation, contrast, and responsive behavior for regressions.

Start with the problem actually visible: hierarchy, type, spacing, imagery, color, or interaction. Stop when the brief is satisfied; do not replace sound sections just to make the diff look comprehensive.

## Review the actual page

Inspect desktop and narrow layouts with real content. Check opening hierarchy, section purpose, image crops, readable line lengths, contrast, and unintended overflow. Exercise navigation, actions, and forms, including touched error and completion states. Review reduced motion and any supported themes affected by the work.

Remove generic filler and decoration that does not help the audience. Report missing assets or unverified claims plainly.
