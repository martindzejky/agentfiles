# Anti-AI-slop

Look for choices that appear regardless of the subject. These are warning signs, not universal bans: the brief, brand, content, or existing system may justify any individual treatment. Fix the cause of sameness rather than replacing one fashionable preset with another.

## Palette and type

- Purple glows and meshes; gradients on every surface; oversized gradient headlines.
- Cream + high-contrast serif + terracotta; near-black + acid green or vermilion; beige artisan palettes applied to unrelated brands.
- A stock “designer” font, or an unfamiliar font chosen only to avoid a familiar one.
- A second typeface, italic word, or accent color in every headline with no semantic reason.
- Uppercase, widely tracked eyebrows above every section; monospace metadata everywhere.

A single well-set family can be enough. Neither pure black/white nor a system font is inherently a failure.

## Structure and decoration

- Every idea in an identical rounded card, regardless of relationship or hierarchy.
- Three feature cards, repeated zigzag rows, or a bento grid chosen before understanding the content.
- Forced layout variety that makes related items harder to compare.
- Faux-newspaper columns, hairlines, crosshairs, or numbered sections without an organizing job. Numbers imply a sequence; use them when order matters.
- A giant heading beside a detached tiny explanation; oversized blank space that weakens the composition.
- Badges, pills, arrows on every link, middle-dot metadata, or decorative labels used as visual filler.
- Custom cursors, every-card hover lifts, repetitive scroll reveals, and ambient loops added by default.

## Imagery and product truth

- Generic emoji icons beside every heading, irrelevant stock images, or decorative illustrations with no connection to the subject.
- Box-drawn dashboards, terminals, or statistics presented as evidence of a product that does not exist. An illustrative mockup is fine when its role is clear and its content is honest.
- Demo-board chrome leaking into shipped UI: viewport selectors, design settings, target-count badges.
- Fake customer logos, testimonials, usage numbers, or precise metrics without evidence.
- Invented values where an honest, clearly identified placeholder would suffice.

## Copy

- Placeholder headings (“Feature One”) or generic brands (“Acme”, “Nexus”, “SmartFlow”) surviving into final work.
- Interchangeable promises such as “unlock your potential” or “seamless experience”; filler verbs such as “elevate”, “unleash”, and “revolutionize”.
- Performative labels (“Field notes”, “Quietly in use at”) unsupported by the content or voice.
- Repeated em dashes, middle-dot strings, and self-conscious editorial asides.
- Visible commentary about the design, framework, or implementation instead of language useful to the audience.

## Review in context

Inspect the rendered artifact. Identify the few choices that make it feel generic, then improve hierarchy, content, or purpose before adding more decoration. Preserve justified brand choices and useful consistent patterns. Do not turn this catalog into another mandatory visual style.
